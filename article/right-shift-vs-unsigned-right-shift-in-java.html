Right Shift vs Unsigned Right Shift in Java

Java provides two right bitwise shift operators: the signed right shift (>>) and the unsigned right shift (>>>). While both operators shift the binary representation of an integer to the right by a specified number of positions, their critical difference lies in how they handle the leftmost vacant positions, known as the sign bit. This article explains the technical mechanics of both operators, highlights their behavior with positive and negative numbers, and outlines when to use each.

The Signed Right Shift Operator (>>)

The signed right shift operator (>>), often called the arithmetic right shift, shifts the bits of an integer to the right and fills the vacant positions on the left with a copy of the original sign bit (the most significant bit).

In Java's two's complement binary representation:

  • If the number is positive, the sign bit is 0. Shifting right fills the left positions with 0s.
  • If the number is negative, the sign bit is 1. Shifting right fills the left positions with 1s.

Because the sign bit is preserved, the sign of the operand does not change. Mathematically, x >> n is equivalent to dividing x by \(2^n\) using floor division.

Example:

  • Positive value: 8 >> 1
    • Binary: 0000...0000 1000 shifted right by 1 becomes 0000...0000 0100 (Decimal: 4).
  • Negative value: -8 >> 1
    • Binary: 1111...1111 1000 shifted right by 1 becomes 1111...1111 1100 (Decimal: -4).

The Unsigned Right Shift Operator (>>>)

The unsigned right shift operator (>>>), commonly referred to as the logical right shift, shifts all bits to the right and always fills the leftmost positions with 0, regardless of whether the original number is positive or negative.

Because the leftmost bits are always replaced with 0:

  • For positive numbers, the behavior is identical to >> because the sign bit was already 0.
  • For negative numbers, shifting a 0 into the sign bit converts the value from a negative number into a large positive number.

Example:

  • Positive value: 8 >>> 1
    • Binary: 0000...0000 1000 shifted right by 1 becomes 0000...0000 0100 (Decimal: 4).
  • Negative value: -8 >>> 1
    • Binary: 1111...1111 1000 shifted right by 1 becomes 0111...1111 1100 (Decimal: 2147483644).

Summary of Differences

Feature Signed Right Shift (>>) Unsigned Right Shift (>>>)
Shift Type Arithmetic Logical
Left-fill Bit Sign bit (0 if positive, 1 if negative) Always 0
Positive Numbers Divides by powers of 2 Divides by powers of 2 (identical to >>)
Negative Numbers Preserves negative sign Flips sign bit to 0, yielding a positive value
Primary Use Case Mathematical division by powers of 2 Low-level bit manipulation, hashing, and cryptography
